# Go-to-market: Always Broke Ltd (B2B, sourcing deals)

Marketing here means **reaching owners who want to sell and getting a bank or adviser to vouch for us**. Evidence: `founder/panel-v2/results.md` (20 simulated sellers, Germany as a proxy for the EU27), `founder/competitors.md`, `founder/offer.md`, `founder/cfo.md`. Panel buyers are simulated and results are thin; quotes below are research, never to be used as customer quotes.

**Do not start outreach until the v2 offer is true**: an EU entity with named directors, a bank letter of funds, and a bank-guaranteed deferred part. Today none exist (see `offer.md`). Everything below assumes they will before day 1.

## 1. Who first (from the panel)
- All three main segments took the meeting in 100% of cases (retail/hospitality 5, craft/trade 7, niche B2B 6); realistic close rates are 2%, 1% and 1% and are labelled thin (too few buyers). **No segment is proven better**, so I am not pretending one is.
- Best first audience on the evidence: **owner-managers aged 55+ of craft, trade and niche B2B firms with 8+ staff** (all 100% meeting rate in those groups). Owner-dependent micro firms (50% meeting) and "family or management buyout preferred" (50%) are the weakest.
- **Who really decides:** the owner, but 17 of 20 said they would wait for their own bank, tax adviser or lawyer to vouch. The adviser and the bank are the second audience.

## 2. Positioning
For **owner-managers of profitable small businesses with no successor**, who want **their staff, name and site to carry on and the money to be safe**, Always Broke is the **permanent buyer that pays 90% in cash at closing with written job, site and name guarantees**, unlike a **broker-run sale to the highest bidder or a financial investor who resells in a few years**.

Three versions:
1. **Continuity-led (recommended):** the above. Supported by the panel: staff, name and site guarantees were what the meeting-takers cared about most (P002, P012, P018).
2. **Trust-led:** "A buyer your own adviser and bank can check before you sign." Supported by the top flip condition: own adviser and bank confirming funds and directors.
3. **Cash-led:** "90% cash at closing, the rest bank-guaranteed." Supported by price-led sellers (3 of 20) and the 10% holdback objection; risks the commodity trap (competing only on price).

**Per role:**
- Owner (the return): "Your life's work stays whole, your staff keep their jobs for 24 months, 90% is paid in cash at closing."
- Staff and managers (the daily difference): "Same management, same name, same site. No restructuring."
- Seller's adviser and bank (risk and terms): "Named EU directors, a bank letter of funds shown before signing, the deferred 10% bank-guaranteed, no re-pricing except for undisclosed material problems."

## 3. Channels (two to start, one to watch)

Advisers, banks and chambers as referrers** (tax advisers, house banks, chambers of commerce and crafts, succession advisers) | 17 of 20 sellers wait for their own adviser or bank to vouch; every named alternative (adviser, chamber, nexxt-change) runs through them | Founder time; reuses the EUR 20,000 search budget (estimate) | Intros received, adviser calls and bank-letter checks completed |
| **2. Personal letters to owners** (named owner, from a named director; phone follow-up) | Owners decide after meeting the buyer (8 of 20); a letter is the cheapest way to get a first meeting | Print and post per letter; founder time | Meeting rate vs the panel's 24% realistic |

**Not doing:** paid social, content marketing, trade-show stands and cold email. Reasons: no panel evidence they reach this buyer, they cost money the company does not have, and unsolicited email to businesses in Germany generally needs the recipient's consent, so confirm with a lawyer before any email or phone sequence. ## 4. Target account list (to build, not invent)
Build **100 to 272 accounts** by sector and size from public sources only, not from this file: the commercial register, chamber directories, trade association member lists, and each firm's own website. Filters: owner aged 55+ where public, 8 to 40 staff, craft, trade or niche B2B, profitable (published accounts where they exist). **I have not named any real company.** Start with one German region and one or two sectors, because the board asked for one-sentence focus; choose them with the founder.

## 5. The funnel, backwards (panel-v2 rates)
Realistic panel rates: **24% take a meeting, about 1.2% close** (12 of 20 "might" x 2%).
- **One deal needs about 83 accounts approached and about 20 meetings** (1 / 0.012 and 83 x 0.24).
- **Three deals need about 250 approaches and about 60 meetings.** The panel's own run: 272 approached, 65 meetings, about 3 closings, all landing in year 2.
- **Selling time:** 65 meetings a year is about 1.3 a week: feasible for one founder.
- **Sales cycle:** sellers said 12 to 15 months; the CFO's month-4 first deal is above this. Plan the first closing for **month 13 or later**.

## 6. The 90-day plan (week by week)
| Weeks | What | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Make the preconditions real (EU entity, bank letter, bank guarantee); choose region and sectors; build the first 100 accounts | Entity and letter in hand; account list |
| 3-4 | Meet 10 advisers, banks and chambers: ask for a call with their own contacts, show the bank letter and structure | 3 adviser or bank introductions |
| 5-8 | Send 25 personal letters a week (100 total); phone follow-up 7 days later; log everything | 100 approached; reply rate |
| 9-12 | Hold meetings and site visits; ask each owner's adviser to call our bank and legal adviser; Friday pipeline review | 15 to 25 meetings; list of owners who "might" |

**Friday review:** accounts approached, meetings booked, adviser or bank checks completed.

## 7. Ten hooks (each answers one panel objection; use only once the claim is true)
1. **"No track record."** "We are new. Here is our bank, our legal adviser and named directors: call them before you decide."
2. **"Belize shell."** "Your buyer is an EU-registered company with named directors you can meet."
3. **"No proof of funds."** "A bank letter confirming funds for the full price is shown to your adviser before you sign."
4. **"30% deferred, unsecured."** "90% in cash at closing; the other 10% within 12 months, bank-guaranteed."
5. **"What about my staff?"** "Jobs and site guaranteed in writing for 24 months. Your name stays."
6. **"Others will pay more."** "Compare any offer on cash at closing, certainty and what happens to your people, not only the multiple."
7. **"My adviser would pick it apart."** "Please have them. We contribute up to EUR 5,000 to their costs."
8. **"Will you resell?"** "If we ever sell, you and your staff get the first right to buy it back, for ten years."
9. **"Will they cut the price later?"** "No re-pricing after due diligence except for material problems you did not disclose."
10. **"I am tired of the process."** "A confidential first meeting costs nothing. After closing you stay for a paid handover of up to 12 months."

When there is nothing new to say: share a short written summary of how a purchase works (steps, who signs, who checks), written for the seller's adviser, with no names or numbers from real sellers.

## 8. Budget and cost to win
- **Budget:** the CFO's EUR 20,000 search and pipeline estimate (not confirmed). Split: letters and printing about 30%, adviser meetings and travel about 40%, tools and research about 30% (my assumption, to be replaced with quotes).
- **Cost per approach:** EUR 20,000 / 272 = about EUR 73. **Cost per closed deal:** EUR 20,000 / about 3.3 deals = about EUR 6,000 (my arithmetic, one founder's unpaid time not counted).
- **Payback:** the CFO's net contribution is EUR 7,500 a month, so EUR 6,000 is earned back in under one month **once a deal is closed**; the cost is real in year 1 and the income only in year 2.
- **Most we can pay to win a deal:** well above EUR 6,000; the limit is the funding, not the cost to win.

## 9. Three numbers to watch weekly
1. **Meeting rate** among accounts approached. Panel realistic is 24%. If it is **below 10% after 100 approaches**, change the message (my rule of thumb).
2. **Adviser or bank checks completed** per meeting. If advisers refuse to take the call, the trust fix is not working: pause outreach and fix that first.
3. **Months from first meeting to a signed LOI.** Sellers said 12 to 15 months; if the first LOI takes longer than 15 months, review price and terms.

## Rules kept
No fake testimonials, references or follower counts; no claim that is not true; no real company named; paid partnerships (e.g. adviser fees) disclosed; comply with advertising and unsolicited-contact rules (Germany: unfair competition and data protection law). Not legal advice.
