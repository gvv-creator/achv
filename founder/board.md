## 2. Risks raised by more than one member (most dangerous first)
1. **The ask grew while the evidence did not** (Account, Monopoly). v2 asked for €250k released against gates. v3 asks the board to commit €450k of financing capacity and delegate G0–G3 to management, with the first board review at M6, by which point about €128k has been spent net of receipts.
2. **Delay is the most likely failure, and it is not funded** (Account, Monopoly). Payroll runs from M1 whatever HEVADEX does. A three-month slip needs about €227k of facility against €200k, and the facility is assumed, not offered.
3. **The value hurdle is unmeasured** (Offers, Product). The customer pays about €18.96/m² against an assumed €12/m²; the offer needs ≥€6.96/m² of avoided damage. Factory film on frames, summer application limits and the manufacturer's exclusion of protected-surface damage all work against it.
4. **Repeat orders carry the whole case** (Offers, Account). Acquisition costs about €4.6k per account against about €4.9k first-year contribution, and three-year cumulative EBITDA is –€6.6k with no return case until M12.

### New issues the board found in v3
- **Seasonality.** OMNIGUARD 110NP may not be applied in full sun or on surfaces above 30°C. If M1 is December, the ramp's steepest months (M7–M11) are June to October.
- **The technical veto is not independent.** The commercial and technical lead is one person; the person who approves a substrate is the person who needs the order.
- **Insurance is the lead's real gate.** HEVADEX excludes protected-surface damage. If no insurer covers it, the lead application cannot be sold safely.
- **Quote throughput.** €180k needs about 150 surveyed quotes, about 17 a month, from the same lead who sells and runs discovery.
- **Equity capacity is unshown.** Whether ACHV's contracting business can provide €250k without straining its own liquidity is a Phase 1 deliverable, not something the board has seen.

### What v3 fixed from the v2 conditions
G4 moved to M10; cash model reconciled with the P&L, VAT added; the who-pays question in every interview; the cost-bearing contractor as the priority buyer; a bounded, insured pilot remedy; scheduled removal and heat controls; substrate exclusions; backup supervisor by M6; two-pilot cap; the BLOWERPROOF pallet target to be renegotiated; a direct peelable competitor identified; pricing corrected to cover QA and claims. Still open, because they need the outside world: signed scope including OMNIGUARD, supplier prices, local damage measurements, pilots and LOIs.

### Where the board still disagrees
- **How much to approve now.** The Account and Monopoly lenses want only a capped Phase 1a released until G0 and G1 pass. The Product and Offers lenses would accept the full programme mandate if the operating clock starts at G0 and the seasonality and insurance conditions are met.
- **Lead application.** The Product lens still backs OMNIGUARD for visible proof; the Offers lens still thinks the bigger pain is roof/podium waterproofing and wants factory-film prevalence and summer limits counted before the lead is fixed.

## 4. The strongest version the board can see
The design is now close to the board's strongest version; the gap is sequence and evidence. The strongest version approves the programme in principle but spends almost nothing until the supplier signs a scope that includes OMNIGUARD, an insurer will cover protected-surface damage, and fifteen interviews have measured who pays for handover damage and how much. Payroll, stock and capex start the day those three things exist. That turns the plan's most likely failure (a slow supplier) from a €227k facility problem into a few weeks of desk work, and lets the board vote on the €450k with the one thing every version has lacked: an answer from the market.

## 5. B2B open questions for the account panel and the CFO
- **Who pays and how much:** measured damage and conventional protection cost per m² on Maltese jobs; does it sit with the glazing, main contractor or developer?
- **Substitute:** share of frames arriving with factory film, and how long it stays on.
- **Sales cycle and throughput:** surveyed quotes per month one person can produce; quote-to-order conversion.
- **Repeat rate:** share of pilot accounts reordering within six months.
- **Payment terms:** deposit acceptance and actual payment days (model: two-month lag, 2% loss).
- **Supplier terms:** HEVADEX credit after prepaid orders, MOQ, lead time and current prices for the three launch products.
- **Seasonality:** usable application hours by month on sun-facing elevations.
- **Concentration:** largest external customer share, and ACHV own-site share, at €180k.
