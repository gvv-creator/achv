## The idea

- What it is: A newly incorporated Belize investment holding company that acquires and holds small and micro niche businesses across the EU, long term, in the Swedish investment-company style.
- Who it is for: Two counterparties. (1) Sellers: owner-managed EU small/micro businesses facing succession or needing capital and management support. (2) Funders: whoever provides initial and later capital (not yet identified).
- What it sells, at what price: Not a product sale. It offers sellers a permanent home (continuity of management and culture, liquidity or succession) and offers funders compounding returns from cash-generative businesses. Confirmed offer terms (founder, 2026-11-10): 3.5x to 4.5x EBITDA, 90% cash at closing, 10% within 12 months bank-guaranteed, adviser contribution capped at EUR 5,000. Funder fees and return targets: not yet defined.
- Where and how: Holding company in Belize; targets in the EU; first deal is an independently sourced third-party business meeting defined financial, commercial and risk criteria. Pipeline not yet built. Funding not yet secured.
- Model: B2B (other: M&A / investment holding; counterparties are business owners and capital providers).
- Budget and constraints: Not stated by the founder; the CFO scenario shows about EUR 0.9m for one EUR 200k-EBITDA business. Startup stage; governance, initial funding and pipeline all still to be established. Conservative borrowing; no fixed exit timetable.

## What the board said

### 1. The vote
**4 x FUND IF, 0 x FUND, 0 x PASS.** Scores: Offers 3, Monopoly 3, Product 3, Account 4. **Average 3.25 / 10.**

Read it plainly: no member would fund today, and none passed the idea itself. The strategy is plausible; the company as described has no evidence yet.

### 2. Risks raised by more than one member (go first)
1. **No track record, team, funding or pipeline: nothing makes the promise believable.** (all four). The offer to sellers ("a permanent home") only works if sellers and funders believe you will close.
2. **The Belize structure.** (all four). Sellers, banks, notaries and funders may see it as a trust, KYC/AML, tax and recourse problem. Nobody on the board knows the actual legal position; it is an open question for EU legal and tax advisers, not a conclusion.
3. **The funder side is undefined.** (Offers, Account, Monopoly, Product). Funders, return targets, fees, structure and the minimum raise for deal one are unknown.
4. **No named secret or focus; crowded buyer side.** (Monopoly, Product, Offers). The filter "niche, defensible, cash-generative, any EU country" is not a focus. Brokers, search funds, PE, family offices and other holding companies chase the same sellers; no sourcing channel is named.
5. **Unproven claim that continuity beats price for sellers.** (Offers, Monopoly, Account). Untested; if wrong, this is a commodity offer at a lower price.

### 3. Conditions (merged checklist)
- [ ] Written funder offer: who funds, target return, fees, term, structure, protections, minimum raise for deal one.
- [ ] At least one named funder with written, conditional commitment sized for deal one.
- [ ] Named, credible team and/or operator/sector expert with relevant record.

- [ ] Written sourcing channel with evidence of real owner conversations; documented pipeline, not just names.
- [ ] At least one letter of intent or named seller proving the seller offer has demand.
- [ ] Legal and tax opinion on the Belize holding (EU regulatory treatment, banking, AML/KYC, capital raising); re-domicile if unfavourable. A working bank account or lender indicated.
- [ ] Written target criteria: financial, commercial, risk, including customer-concentration ceiling and owner-dependence test.
- [ ] Seller-facing offer in writing: what management and culture continuity means in practice; deal structure (cash, deferred, earn-out, rollover) and indicative timeline.
- [ ] Comparison against rival buyers showing why a seller would choose this one.
- [ ] Open unknowns closed: budget, purchase multiples, fees, size band, competitor map.

### 4. The strongest version of this business
The board sees a **narrow, funded, named-operator acquirer**: one country and one or two sectors, a fixed size band, a stated concentration ceiling, committed capital for deal one, and a structure (probably EU-domiciled or EU-opined) that a seller's lawyer accepts in two minutes. The real differentiator is permanent ownership with continuity, backed by deal terms that prove it (retained management, rollover or earn-out). That differs from the pitch: the founder pitched a general EU holding company with the structure and funding still open. The board's version starts as a specialist with one credible first deal and earns the group form afterwards.

### 5. B2B open questions (for the account panel and the CFO)

- **Payment terms:** what structures will sellers accept (cash at close, deferred, earn-out, rollover)? What do funders expect (equity, loan, fund, family money)?
- **Concentration:** customer, supplier, staff and owner-dependence limits for targets; concentration of funder capital in one or two backers.

### 3. Positioning map (text)

### 6. The threat

Lagercrantz says it contacts "at least 100 companies each year" (via a search summary), so deal-flow machines already exist.
- **Structural threat:** the buyer who can show funds and a track record wins contested deals; Always Broke has neither yet.
- **Also note:** the Commission's June 2026 recommendation calls for matchmaking platforms and lower barriers to cross-border transfers, which could make sellers easier for everyone to find.

**C. Adviser and bank check (the main trust step)**

**D. From indicative offer to closing**

**E. A seller's concern or complaint**
1. Listen, repeat it back. 2. Reply within 2 working days with the specific answer or the next step. 3. Put anything promised in writing. 4. Never offer a term not in the approved offer.

**F. Monthly review after closing**
1. Receive the month's numbers by the 10th. 2. Compare to the same month last year. 3. Call the manager; ask what they need. 4. Log deferred payment due dates and the guarantee. 5. Raise any deviation of more than 10% in cash or sales with the adviser (my rule of thumb).

Accounts approached, meetings, adviser checks completed, LOIs; compare with the panel's 24% meeting rate (`marketing.md`).
