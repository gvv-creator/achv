# Operations: Always Broke Ltd (B2B, acquirer)

Here "running the business" means: sourcing and closing a deal, then overseeing what you bought. Inputs: `idea.md`, `offer.md`, `marketing.md`, `cfo.md`, `numbers.json`. Every legal and tax point below is something to **confirm with a lawyer, notary or tax adviser**; I could only run public searches. Not legal advice. Germany is the proxy region (see `customer.json`).

## 1. The cycle, step by step (what the seller sees is marked **S**)
**Per deal (sourcing to closing):**
1. Build and refresh the target list from public registers (`marketing.md` section 4).
2. Send the personal letter (**S**), phone follow-up 7 days later (**S**).
3. First confidential meeting at the seller's site (**S**); sign a mutual NDA.
4. Seller's adviser and bank check us: bank letter of funds, directors, entity, guarantee (**S**).
5. Indicative offer within the confirmed 3.5x to 4.5x EBITDA range, 90% cash at closing, 10% within 12 months bank-guaranteed (**S**).
6. Letter of intent with an exclusivity period (**S**).
7. Due diligence by our lawyer, accountant and tax adviser (legal, tax, financial, commercial) (**S**: they supply documents).
8. Share purchase agreement; **notarisation is mandatory for GmbH shares** ([Hamburg Chamber of Commerce FAQ](https://www.handelskammer-hamburg.de/recht-steuern/wirtschaftssrecht/unternehmensgruendung/unternehmenskauf-haeufige-fragen-6684992); a share deal needs no separate asset transfers but takes on all liabilities, per [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/insights/unternehmenskauf-share-deal-versus-asset-deal/)) (**S**).
9. Closing: cash paid, guarantee issued, management and staff meeting (**S**).
10. 12-month paid handover (**S**); deferred 10% paid and guarantee released.

**After closing (monthly and quarterly):** monthly numbers from the business (sales, cash, headcount), quarterly review with its manager, annual accounts check, deferred payment diary, covenant and guarantee tracker.

**Weekly rhythm (one founder):** see section 3.

## 3. People and the weekly rota
**Roles:**
- **Founder:** sourcing, meetings, seller relationship, pipeline.
- **Named directors of the EU entity:** the offer promises named directors sellers can check. **Who they are is an open question** (not in the files).
- **Lawyer, tax adviser, bank contact, notary:** outside advisers.
- **The target's own manager:** stays on after closing; guaranteed jobs for 24 months.

**Weekly rota, first 12 weeks (my assumption: one founder, about 25 hours):**
| Day | What | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Write and post 25 letters; log in the pipeline sheet | 4 |
| Tue | Adviser, bank and chamber meetings | 5 |
| Wed | Phone follow-ups (7 days after each letter) | 4 |
| Thu | Owner meetings and site visits (about 1 or 2 a week on average from `marketing.md`) | 8 |
| Fri | Pipeline review; update adviser contacts | 4 |

**Slow first month:** weeks 1 to 4 are mostly the preconditions (entity, bank letter) and the first adviser meetings, so the founder's letters start in week 5 (`marketing.md`).
**Wage assumption:** the CFO scenario has no founder salary. Founder time is unpaid and not in the numbers; flag for the founder. Employer costs and payroll vary by country: flag for the accountant.

## 4. Routines (one page each)
**A. First letter and call**
1. Pull the owner's name and firm from the register. 2. Send a one-page letter signed by a named director. 3. Phone after 7 days: ask one question ("can I meet you for 30 minutes?"). 4. Log: sent, answered, meeting booked, reason for no.

**B. First meeting**
1. Meet at the owner's site, not ours. 2. Ask what matters: staff, name, site, money, timing. 3. State plainly what we have and do not have (no track record yet; bank letter available). 4. Leave nothing to sign except an NDA. 5. Same day: send a short note and ask who their adviser and bank are.

**C. Adviser and bank check (the main trust step)**
1. Send the owner's adviser: entity extract, named directors, bank letter of funds, guarantee draft. 2. Offer a call with our bank and our lawyer. 3. Answer every question in writing within 2 working days. 4. Record each doubt and what closed it.

**D. From indicative offer to closing**
1. Indicative offer in writing (range, 90/10, no re-pricing clause, guarantees). 2. LOI with exclusivity. 3. Due diligence checklist from our lawyer; seller supplies documents. 4. If a material issue appears, say so in writing and renegotiate once. 5. Notarised SPA. 6. Closing: cash, guarantee, staff meeting.

**E. A seller's concern or complaint**
1. Listen, repeat it back. 2. Reply within 2 working days with the specific answer or the next step. 3. Put anything promised in writing. 4. Never offer a term not in the approved offer.

**F. Monthly review after closing**
1. Receive the month's numbers by the 10th. 2. Compare to the same month last year. 3. Call the manager; ask what they need. 4. Log deferred payment due dates and the guarantee. 5. Raise any deviation of more than 10% in cash or sales with the adviser (my rule of thumb).

**G. Friday pipeline review**
Accounts approached, meetings, adviser checks completed, LOIs; compare with the panel's 24% meeting rate (`marketing.md`).

## 7. Risk register
| # | What goes wrong | Likelihood | Impact | Plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | No funder commits | High | Fatal | Do not start outreach before the bank letter exists; target funders first |
| 2 | Banks refuse accounts or financing because of the Belize link | Medium to high | High | Lawyer opinion; EU entity first; ask the bank before formation |
| 3 | No seller signs; no references | High | High | Adviser and bank vouching; first deal at a price that wins; accept year-2 closings |
| 4 | Price-led sellers take a broker's higher offer | High | Medium | Compete on cash certainty, staff guarantees; do not outbid |
| 5 | Key person leaves after the owner (owner-dependence) | Medium | High | Owner-dependence test in target criteria; paid 12-month handover; retention terms |
| 6 | Largest customer of the target leaves after the succession | Medium | High | Concentration ceiling in target criteria; speak to the main customer before signing |
| 7 | Due diligence misses a liability (share deal takes on all liabilities) | Medium | High | Full diligence by lawyer and tax adviser; price protection in the SPA |
| 8 | A big customer of the target pays late | Medium | Medium | Check ageing in diligence; working-capital buffer from the CFO |
| 9 | Founder overloaded; single point of failure | High | Medium | Named directors and advisers; cap letters at the planned weekly volume |
| 10 | Funder pooling or the Belize link triggers regulation | Low to medium (unknown) | Very high | Lawyer's written opinion before taking any funder's money |
| 11 | A supplier fails a large order at the target | Low | Medium | Supplier concentration check in diligence |
