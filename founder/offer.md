# The offer: Always Broke Ltd

Offer name: **The Permanent Home Agreement** (working name).

Method: the Offers lens (a summary of a published framework; not anyone's words). Inputs: `founder/panel/results.md` (v1: 20 of 20 sellers said no, all for trust), `founder/numbers.json`, `founder/board.md`.

**Terms confirmed by the founder (2026-11-10):** the 3.5x to 4.5x EBITDA multiple, the 90% cash at close / 10% within 12 months split, and the EUR 5,000 adviser contribution cap are confirmed. The EUR 2,000 guarantee set-up cost and other costs remain my estimates.

**Read this first.** The v1 panel said no to the *company*, not to the price: a Belize shell with no track record, no committed funding and 30% of the price deferred. An offer cannot fix that by wording alone. Two of the fixes below (the EU entity with named directors and legal opinion, and the bank letter of funds) are *preconditions the founder must actually put in place*, and the bank-guaranteed deferred part depends on them. The v2 pitch describes the offer **as if those exist**, so the re-test shows what changes when trust is fixed. It is a scenario, not the company today, and nothing in the v2 pitch may be sent to a real seller until each claim is true.

## 3. The stack

- **Core:** a permanent home for the business, with named buyer you can check, full price in cash at close (90%), and a written job, site and name guarantee.
- **Risk reversal (trust):** bank letter of funds shown to your adviser before you sign; the deferred 10% secured by a bank guarantee; buy-back right if we resell.
- **Easy to approve:** adviser contribution; calls with our bank and legal adviser; plain terms your adviser can read in an hour.
- **Easy to switch:** paid handover role up to 12 months.
- **Urgency or scarcity:** none. A succession has real urgency (owner age), but I am not adding any countdown. Always Broke has no limited slots to offer.

## 4. Costs (CFO tool, `founder/numbers.json` plus changes)

Changes from the CFO scenario: 90% paid at close (EUR 702,000), plus EUR 5,000 adviser contribution and EUR 2,000 guarantee/letter set-up (both estimates), deal costs and search as before.
- Cash needed before it pays for itself: **EUR 806,400** (was EUR 877,400), because less of the price is paid at close.
- Plus the **EUR 78,000 deferred payment** due within 12 months, so about **EUR 884,400** in total (my arithmetic). That is only EUR 7,000 more than v1.
- Not modelled: the guarantee's bank fee beyond the EUR 2,000 estimate, legal fees for the EU structure, and the seller's paid handover (assumed to sit inside the target's own costs; check against the real accounts).
- Year 1 operating profit unchanged at EUR 52,500 in this scenario.

## 6. Re-test

Same 20 sellers (seed 357363), same `customer.json`, new pitch (`pitch.md`; old one in `pitch-v1.md`). Results: `founder/panel-v2/results.md` (v1 kept in `founder/results-v1.md`). Remember the v2 pitch describes an EU entity, bank letter of funds and bank-guaranteed deferred payment that **do not exist yet**: this tests what trust fixes would buy, it does not describe today's company.

| | v1 | v2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Would take a meeting (stated) | 5% (1 of 20) | 95% (19 of 20) |
| Takes a meeting (realistic) | 1% | 24% |
| Signs within 12 months (stated, "probably" or "definitely") | 0% | 0% |
| Signs within 12 months (realistic) | 0% | 1% |
| Intent: might / probably not / definitely not | 0 / 2 / 18 | 12 / 7 / 1 |
| Realistic meetings from the 272 owners reached in year 1 | 3 | 65 |
| Realistic deals closed in year 1 | 0.0 | 0.0 (cycles of 12 to 15 months push closings into year 2) |
| Main reason for not signing | trust, 20 of 20 | trust 17, price 3 |

**What moved:** the free first meeting is now an easy yes (95% against 5%), because the named directors, bank letter and written job, site and name guarantees answered most of the first-round objections. Eighteen of 20 sellers went from "definitely not" to "might" or "probably not".

**What did not move:** nobody would sign. The objection that remains, in 17 sellers' words, is "no track record, no earlier sellers to call". That cannot be fixed by wording: it needs a first completed deal, or a credible reference (an adviser or bank the seller already knows vouching for the buyer, as several sellers asked). Three sellers (P001, P013, P020) are price-led and would take the highest all-cash offer from a broker process, and the 10% held back for a year still worries several.

**Caveats:** only 20 simulated buyers, and the meeting rate uses the tool's default 25% calibration for stated meetings (an assumption). The price answers are ambiguous for sellers (the questions are written from a buyer's side), so they should not be used for pricing without a re-read by `/founder-pricing`.

**Verdict:** the offer improved the meeting rate sharply and the sign rate from 0% to about 1%, but it is not enough to sign deals on its own. The bottleneck is the missing first deal and references, not the terms. Next steps for the founder: make the preconditions real (EU entity, committed funders, bank letter), line up bank and adviser vouching, and test with a handful of real owners, using the realistic 24% meeting rate and about 1% close rate as the bar.
