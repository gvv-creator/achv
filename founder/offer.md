## 3. The stack

- **Core:** a documented, signed handover: glazing and frames protected until handover and peeled off on a scheduled date, with the QA pack.
- **Bonuses, each killing one objection:** free test panel and compatibility file (problems 5, 6, 11); damage baseline and business case (4, 13); independence and confidentiality (8).
- **Guarantee:** ACHV pays documented direct damage from the coating or its removal up to €2,500 a job, on listed surfaces and when applied and removed per the method statement. Self-insured for now.
- **Risk reversal:** three founding-site pilots at €7.28/m² (100 to 150 m²), 60 days, no deposit.
- **Urgency and scarcity:** the first three sites only, or until month 6, whichever is first. That is true: pilot margin is near zero and one supervisor is the capacity limit. No countdown.
- **Name:** "Protected Handover, specified line" for the developer or main contractor; "Protected Handover" for the subcontractor.

## 6. Re-test on the account panel (same seed, same customer profile, same 20 buyers)

Same cards (checked identical) as P001 to P020 of the first run, in `panel-v2/`, with the v2 pitch. The "before" figures are the same 20 buyers' original answers.

| | v1 pitch | v2 pitch |
|---|---:|---:|
| Take a meeting | 2 of 20 | **4 of 20** (realistic 2% to 5%) |
| Pay for a pilot | 0 | **0** |
| Realistic close rate | 0% | **0%** |
| "Definitely not" | 6 | **0** (all 20 now "probably not") |
| Deposit as an objection | 13 mentions | **0** |
| Local reference, liability or cap in objections and flip conditions | about 15 | **about 57** |
| Median "too expensive" price | €16.50 | **€20** |
| Median "expensive" price | €12 | **€14** |

What moved: nobody is a hard no any more, the deposit objection disappeared, and meetings doubled (a free test panel is the draw: P002, P010, P011, P015). What did not: **no buyer would pay for a pilot even at the founding-site price of about €14 all-in** (P009 and P002 said so directly), and nothing converts.

What replaced the old objections: **no Maltese reference** (34 mentions) and **the €2,500 cap is too low** for a floor of coated glass (P003, P015, P018, P019, P013 said so). Price tolerance also rose, and it probably did so because the pitch put a €14 anchor next to the €19; that is an anchoring effect of this pitch, not a finding about the market.

Cautions: 20 buyers, not 100; the pitch was written to answer the objections, which makes simulated buyers more agreeable than real ones; and the three buyers who took a meeting did so because it costs them nothing. By the skill's rule, **the offer improves the attitude but the remaining blockers are the market and the proof, not the stack.**

## 7. What the offer does not fix

1. **The price.** Standard price €18.96 all-in is still above the acceptable range (€4 to €14), and ACHV has no margin at the top of that range at €9/kg landed. Only a supplier price near €5/kg landed does (`pricing.md`).
2. **No Maltese reference.** It exists only after the first pilot, and the pilot did not convert in the re-test.
3. **The cap.** €2,500 reads as too low. Test a cap tied to area (for example per m² protected) or a higher cap with the insurer; the cost has to be re-run in the CFO tool before it goes in a pitch.

## 8. Before this pitch is used with a real buyer

- Counsel to approve the capped remedy and the confidentiality undertaking (board condition: capped, insured, counsel-approved).
- Written insurer position or a priced self-insured limit (board G0 item).
- A current dated OMNIGUARD sheet and substrate list from Hevadex, and its answer on cost per kg.
- The three founding-site prices, the €2,500 cap and the 100 m² minimum are ACHV decisions I have assumed; none is signed or quoted. The pitch says only what ACHV could do once those are approved.
