# Offers lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The seller pain is real and named: owner-managed EU small and micro businesses facing succession or needing capital and management support. Succession is a pain with a deadline (the owner's age and energy), which is natural urgency, not fake urgency.
- The dream outcome for a seller is clear and emotionally strong: liquidity, a permanent home, continuity of management, culture and employees. "Not a predetermined resale timetable" is a genuine differentiator against a private-equity buyer who must exit in a few years.
- The offer to sellers is not a price war. Competing on certainty, continuity and fit, rather than the highest multiple, is the right instinct.
- Conservative borrowing lowers the effort and sacrifice a seller bears (less risk of the buyer failing after close).

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proof, so no perceived likelihood.** Likelihood is the weakest term in the value equation. There is no track record, no funding, no pipeline and no first deal. A seller handing over a life's work, and a funder handing over capital, both ask "why believe you will do what you say?" The brief gives no answer. It is also unstated why a Belize holding company is the right counterparty for an EU seller, who may see this as a trust and legal-recourse concern.
2. **The offer to funders is undefined.** Returns, fees, multiples, term and funder type are all "not yet defined". With no stated outcome, there is nothing to score for the funder market: no dream outcome, no time to result, no guarantee. Funders are also not yet identified, so "easy to find and reach" is unknown.
3. **The market case is asserted, not shown.** The brief says "Swedish model" and "niche, defensible, cash-generative" but gives no evidence that sellers choose a permanent holder over a trade buyer, a competing searcher or a management buyout, or that sellers will accept the price a conservative-debt buyer can pay. If the only difference to a seller is a lower price, it is a commodity offer with worse terms.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly is the first seller you would approach, in which country and sector, and why would they pick you over a trade buyer or a higher bidder?
- What do you hand a seller to make the answer yes feel safe: references, a funded commitment letter, named operators, a retained-management arrangement, a seller note or earn-out?
- Why Belize, and what do EU sellers, banks and regulators in the target countries make of it? What are the tax and recourse implications for them?
- Who are the funders (individuals, family offices, institutions), what outcome are they promised, over what period, and what do they get if you fail to find a deal?
- What is the funding amount and the minimum raise before any deal can close? Open: not stated in the brief.
- What are your purchase-multiple range and size band? Open: not defined.
- Who signs for the seller (owner, spouse, co-owners) and what would block a deal (landlord, key customers, licences)?
- What guarantee can you give: exclusivity, price protection, a commitment to keep management and location?
- Could you charge sellers' "peace of mind" more, or are you relying on being cheaper?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- A written offer to funders: target return, fees, term, what funders own, and what protects them. Without it, no score on the funder side is possible.
- At least one signed, non-binding letter of intent or a named seller in a defined sector and country, to prove the seller offer has demand.
- A named, credible operator or advisor base (acquisition, legal and tax in the EU) so perceived likelihood is more than the founder alone.
- A clear answer on the Belize structure and how it is made acceptable to EU sellers, banks and funders.
- Initial funding secured or committed for the first deal, with the stated criteria (financial, commercial, risk) written down and shown to funders.
