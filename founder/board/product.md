# Product lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The stated promise to sellers is one clear idea: a permanent home for the business, with local management and culture kept. For a succession-minded owner this is a real experience, and "no resale timetable" is a differentiator against typical buyers.
- The brief already says no to some things: small and micro businesses, niche positions, EU targets, conservative borrowing, long-term ownership. That is the start of focus.
- The first deal is meant to be independently sourced and judged against defined criteria, which suggests discipline in the first touchpoint with a seller.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Trust gap at first contact.** A seller hands over a life's work and a funder hands over capital, and both meet a newly incorporated Belize company with no track record, no named team, no funding secured and no pipeline. The brief does not say who the people are or who has done this before. The Belize structure is the first thing a counterparty, bank, notary or advisor will see. The brief says nothing on why Belize, who owns it, or how it is explained. Without a convincing answer, the first five minutes feel like risk, not a permanent home.
2. **EU regulatory perception is unaddressed.** A non-EU holding company buying EU businesses and raising money from funders may raise questions on investment-fund and capital-raising rules, anti-money-laundering checks, beneficial ownership transparency and tax-jurisdiction lists. I do not know which rules apply or whether any are triggered. The brief is silent. If a seller's lawyer or a funder's compliance team stumbles on this, the deal stops.
3. **Focus is still generalist.** "Niche, defensible, cash-generating, growth-capable" across any EU country and any sector is a filter, not a focus. The sellers' experience will differ by country, language, sector and legal system, so there is no repeatable experience to design. The one thing the company must do brilliantly is not named, and the pitch needs a paragraph where it should need a sentence.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing you do better for a seller than any other buyer?
- Which countries and sectors will you say no to for the first three deals?
- Why Belize? Who are the owners and directors, and how would you explain it to a Swedish or German seller's lawyer in two minutes?
- What do the founder's and team's track records show in buying or running small businesses? Who else is involved?
- Which EU and local rules apply to your structure and to raising money from funders? Has a lawyer confirmed this?
- Who are the funders you have in mind, and what would they see on day one: reporting, governance, access, fees?
- What happens to a seller after signing: who is the contact, what is promised on management continuity, and how is it written down?
- Where does the experience pass between people (sourcing agent, advisor, lawyer, you) and who owns it?
- Open unknowns: budget, purchase multiples, fees, return targets, deal size, pipeline.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- A named, credible team and a one-sentence focus (sector and country) before any funding.
- A written explanation of the Belize structure with a legal opinion on EU regulatory treatment, or a re-domiciled structure if that opinion is unfavourable.
- A seller-facing offer in writing: what continuity of management and culture means in practice, with the terms.
- One real, evidenced reference point (a letter of intent from a seller or a committed funder) before scaling the story.
