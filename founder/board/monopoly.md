# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The target niche is real: owner-managed EU small/micro businesses facing succession. Large buyers (PE, big holding companies) mostly ignore the smallest deals, so some room may exist at the bottom.
- Permanent ownership (no resale timetable) is a genuine differentiator against PE and search funds, which must exit. For a seller who cares about continuity, that is a reason to choose this buyer. Whether sellers actually weigh it above price is untested.
- Long horizon and conservative borrowing fit durability: the acquired businesses, if truly niche, may hold their own positions.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No secret, no distribution.** The brief names no proprietary sourcing channel, sector expertise or track record. "Independently sourced third-party business" is not a method. Pipeline is not built, and the founder has no stated way to reach the first 100 owners, let alone the first deal. Deal flow decides everything here.
2. **Crowded buyer side.** Search funds, PE, family offices, other holding companies (including Nordic-style compounders) and brokers all pursue the same sellers. Brokers control much of the listed flow and run auctions, which pushes prices up and returns down. "Permanent home" is claimed by others too. The brief does not say who the founder competes with or why a seller picks Always Broke.
3. **Unproven, unfunded, offshore.** Funders are not identified and a track record does not exist, so the group cannot yet offer sellers certainty of closing. A Belize holding company buying EU businesses may meet seller distrust, lender and funder friction, and regulatory or tax questions; I do not know the actual position. Without capital or credibility it loses every contested deal.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about sourcing or running small EU businesses that the brokers, search funds and family offices do not?
- Which country, sector and size band first? What is the small market you would own?
- How will the first 100 owners hear of you, and what will each contact cost? How many off-market conversations exist today?
- Why a seller would pick you over a higher bidder: what is the evidence that continuity beats price for them?
- What stops another holding company copying this strategy in month six?
- Who are the funders, what do they get, and what ownership and control do they take?
- Why Belize, and how will sellers, lenders and EU counterparties view it?
- What does the group do for the acquired businesses that they cannot do alone, and who runs each one after the founder steps back?
- How does one business become a group: what is the shared advantage that grows with each addition?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- A written, specific secret: a defined niche (country, sector, size) and a sourcing channel with evidence of real owner conversations.
- Named, committed first capital or a credible route to it.
- A named operator or sector expert with relevant record.
- A comparison against rival buyers showing why sellers would choose this one.
- A clear answer on the jurisdiction question, from a lawyer.
- Open unknowns to resolve: purchase multiples, return targets, fees, budget, competitor map.
