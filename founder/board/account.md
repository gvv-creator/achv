# Account lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The "accounts" are identifiable in principle: owner-managed EU small businesses facing succession, a real and recurring pool (size not yet sourced).
- Sellers in succession situations often have few alternatives they like (family handover, a trade buyer, a management buyout, closure), so a permanent-home pitch can differentiate on culture and continuity rather than price alone.
- Long hold with no resale timetable removes the exit-deadline pressure that makes funders and sellers wary of financial buyers.
- Conservative borrowing is stated, which limits dependence on one lender.
- First deal is meant to meet defined criteria, which suggests discipline over speed.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No funder, no deal.** The funder is the first account and the one who signs first. None is identified, and return targets, fees, structure (equity, loan, fund, family money) and cycle are undefined. Without committed capital, sellers will not engage seriously, and an unfunded buyer loses to funded ones. The Belize vehicle, a track record of none and unknown funder switching cost against alternatives (invest in funds with a record) make this the hardest sale.
2. **Banking, AML/KYC and credibility friction.** A new Belize holding buying EU companies may meet extra due diligence from EU banks, lenders, notaries and sellers' advisers, and possible difficulty opening accounts or financing acquisitions. Whether Belize is acceptable to these parties, how beneficial ownership is evidenced and what tax or regulatory classification applies are open questions for legal and tax advisers. This could add months, cost or outright refusals.
3. **Concentration inside the target.** Micro businesses commonly depend on the owner personally and on a few customers, suppliers or staff. After a succession exit, the key customers may leave with the owner. Price and structure (earn-out, seller note, retained stake) could mitigate this, but none is defined.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who are the first three funders, who signs, and what did they say? Any letter of intent or soft commitment?
- Why would a funder back this over an established listed investment company or fund?
- What return, fee and structure terms are you offering funders, and what is the minimum capital for deal one?
- How long from first seller contact to completion do you expect, and how many sellers have you spoken with?
- What do sellers do otherwise (family, trade sale, closure), and why choose you?
- What deal structures will you offer (cash at close, deferred payment, earn-out, rollover)? What are sellers' payment expectations?
- What customer concentration limit will you set for a target?
- Have EU advisers confirmed that a Belize holding is workable for banking, lending, KYC and tax? Which jurisdictions and which banks have you approached?
- Who runs each business after the owner leaves, and what retains them?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- At least one named funder with written, conditional commitment sized for deal one.
- Written adviser opinion (legal and tax, in the target country) on Belize holding structure, banking and AML/KYC, with a working bank account or lender indicated.
- Defined target criteria including a customer concentration ceiling and owner-dependence test.
- A seller offer with deal structure terms and an indicative timeline.
- A documented pipeline of conversations, not names alone.
