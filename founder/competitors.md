# Competitors: Always Broke Ltd (EU27, acquiring small and micro businesses)

Model is B2B (M&A), so "competitors" are other buyers, other ways a seller can exit, and the channels that find sellers. Reads of public pages on 2026-11-10. Full rows are in `competitors.csv`. Review ratings and counts do not exist for this market (n/a). Prices are multiples or "quote only". Nothing here was invented; where a page could not be read, it is listed as unverified below.

## 2. Price range (what a buyer pays)
Prices are multiples of EBITDA, not list prices.
- **Lowest:** about 3.9x average at EUR 200k EBITDA ([DealSuite European M&A Monitor](https://www.dealsuite.com/en/blogs/european-m-a-valuation-gap-closes), undated, advisers' mid-market sample).
- **Highest:** about 7.2x at EUR 10m EBITDA (same source).
- **Median:** no source gives one. Search-fund multiples were not found for Europe (the 7.0x figure I saw is US/Canada, not EU, so not used).
Micro targets therefore price lower than the mid-market, which helps a conservative-debt buyer, but the sample is advisers' own and the date is unknown.

## 3. Positioning map (text)
Axes: **size of business bought** (micro to mid) and **how permanent the offer is**.

- Large and permanent: Lifco, Indutrade, Addtech, Lagercrantz (SEK 50-500m sales at Indutrade; Lagercrantz and Lifco do not state a floor).
- Mid and permanent: Newport & Co (EUR 10-100m revenue), Aethris ("small and mid-cap").
- Small, time-limited: search funds (median about USD 8m revenue, built to exit).
- Micro (under about EUR 1m EBITDA): served mainly by brokers, public exchanges like nexxt-change, or by no buyer at all. **This is the least crowded cell, and also the one where the board found no evidence yet of deals.**

## 4. Complaints about incumbents (themes)
No customer-review sources exist for buyers of businesses, so there are **no counted complaint themes** here and I am not inventing any. The nearest honest evidence is design-level: search funds are built to exit; listed acquirers state minimum sizes (Indutrade) that exclude micro businesses. Seller complaints need interviews, which `/founder-consumer` can simulate and the founder must test with real owners.

## 5. The gap
**Possible gap, not proven:** the micro end (under roughly EUR 1m EBITDA / well below SEK 50m sales). The named permanent buyers either state higher minimums (Indutrade) or say "small and mid-cap" (Aethris); only the Commission's page evidences supply of sellers, and says "millions of companies" without a count. Nobody I read offers a permanent home specifically to owner-dependent micro businesses. Whether sellers want it, and whether such businesses have enough durable cash flow to justify it, is not shown by any source.

## 6. The threat
- **Fastest copier:** an existing permanent holding company (Aethris, Newport & Co, Lifco-type) moving down in size, or a search fund that decides to hold; both already have funding, a team and a seller pitch. Lagercrantz says it contacts "at least 100 companies each year" (via a search summary), so deal-flow machines already exist.
- **Structural threat:** the buyer who can show funds and a track record wins contested deals; Always Broke has neither yet.
- **Also note:** the Commission's June 2026 recommendation calls for matchmaking platforms and lower barriers to cross-border transfers, which could make sellers easier for everyone to find.
